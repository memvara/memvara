# Role-aware selection at a 720-token budget: three arms on the same 199 questions

## 1. The answer

**Routed selection at a 720-token budget holds the control's accuracy, and the falsifier did
not fire.** Routed scored 171 of 199 (85.9%) against the control's 172 of 199 (86.4%) — a
paired difference of -0.5 points, 95% CI [-5.0, +4.0], McNemar exact p = 1 — while cutting the
mean rendered context from 4,048 tokens to 630. That is an 84.4% cut in context and a 77.9%
cut in whole prompts, for a difference in accuracy that the data cannot distinguish from zero.

The falsifier was the user-only arm. It is identical to routed except for one rule: it never
sends a question that asks what the assistant said to assistant turns. If the role model were
wrong — if user turns alone were enough — user-only would have matched routed. It scored 151
of 199 (75.9%), 10.1 points below routed, 95% CI [+5.0, +15.1], p = 0.00032. **The role model
survives its own falsification test.** None of the four pre-registered stop rules fired.

Two limits bound how far that goes. **The control ran through a different gateway than the two
arms**, so every control-versus-arm number carries a confound that these files cannot measure;
the routed-versus-user-only comparison does not carry it. And **199 paired questions cannot
certify non-inferiority at a 3-point margin.** They can rule out a large regression, not a
small one: 199 pairs give an 80% chance of detecting a drop of about 8 points, and a 3-point
margin needs roughly 490 paired questions before a dead-even result would even clear it.
Section 8 works that out.

## 2. Provenance, and what was corrected

All three runs answer and judge with the recorded reader/judge id `gpt-5.4`. The control
started 2026-09-02 and **completed 2026-09-03** (checkpoint `createdAt` 2026-09-02T11:28:03Z;
its answer phase completed between 07:21:48Z and 07:24:20Z on the 3rd). The two arms answered
on 2026-09-04, routed at 07:22–07:27Z and user-only at 07:36–07:41Z.

**Gateways.** The control ran through a different gateway than the two arms, which ran through
`api.llmapi.ai`. That attribution comes from how the runs were set up, not from these files:
`local/arms/run_arm.sh` reads `OPENAI_BASE_URL` from the harness's `.env.local` and never
prints it, and no checkpoint field records it. A reader difference between gateways is
therefore not measurable here. The consequence is asymmetric and worth stating plainly:
routed-versus-control comparisons carry the confound, routed-versus-user-only comparisons do
not, because both arms ran minutes apart through the same gateway.

Every number below was re-derived from the checkpoints by code run for this report:

```
python3 local/analysis/recheck/analyse.py     # a copy of analyse.py, writing into recheck/
diff  local/analysis/results.md local/analysis/recheck/results.md   # identical
python3 local/analysis/verify/verify.py
python3 local/analysis/verify/decompose.py
python3 local/analysis/verify/extra.py
python3 local/analysis/verify/final_checks.py
python3 local/analysis/verify/goldmiss.py
python3 local/analysis/recheck/power.py
```

`verify.py` is written independently of `analyse.py` — from the checkpoints and from the
harness's own TypeScript — and reproduces every figure. Three things are **corrected** here
against the first write-up, and are used in corrected form throughout:

1. **Retrieved items are not all turns.** Both arms retrieve exactly 200 *items* per question,
   of which 189–200 are turns (mean 198.1); the remainder are extracted claims, which
   `turns_only=true` drops before rendering. The earlier text called all 200 turns.
2. **The control completed on 2026-09-03**, having started on the 2nd. The earlier text gave
   only the completion date without the span.
3. **A reader noise floor is now measured**, and it changes how the paired tests read. It was
   missing before. Section 3 has it.

The control retrieves 15 turns per question plus a mean of 3.3 extracted claims and renders
all of both — no budget, no role filter, claims kept. Both arms retrieve 200 items and render
a mean of 9.47 (routed) and 10.53 (user-only) turns under the budget.

**The prompts are known, not assumed.** The checkpoint stores what retrieval returned, not what
the reader saw, so the harness's prompt builder was transliterated and then made to prove

arm's recorded `basePromptTokens`, `promptTokens` and `contextTokens`. Exactly one
configuration survives per arm, on all 199 questions, with zero mismatches — the three rows
above. The control's settings are identified by the data rather than inferred.

## 4. Paired comparisons on the same 199 questions

| comparison | wins | losses | ties | difference | 95% CI (paired bootstrap) | McNemar exact p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| routed vs control | 11 | 12 | 176 | -0.5 pp | [-5.0, +4.0] pp | 1 |
| user-only vs control | 11 | 32 | 156 | -10.6 pp | [-17.1, -4.5] pp | 0.00191 |
| routed vs user-only | 25 | 5 | 169 | +10.1 pp | [+5.0, +15.1] pp | 0.000325 |

Wins are questions the first arm got right and the second got wrong. The interval is the 2.5th
and 97.5th percentile of 10,000 paired bootstrap draws over the 199 questions, seed 20260903.
The p-value is the two-sided exact binomial on the discordant pairs; two independent
implementations and `scipy.stats.binomtest` agree to within 1e-12. The p = 1 for routed versus
control is exact arithmetic, not rounding: 23 discordant pairs is odd, so P(X ≤ 11) = 0.5
exactly under Bin(23, 0.5).

### The reader is not deterministic, and the floor is measurable

The two arms share retrieval byte-for-byte on all 199 questions. Their prompts differ only
where the assistant-question rule fires, which is on 19 questions. **On the other 180 the two
arms sent a byte-identical prompt to the same reader, and the two runs disagree on 14 of
them** — a same-input discordance rate of 7.8%. This is expected: the harness's model registry
has no entry for `gpt-5.4`, so it falls through to a rule marking that family as not accepting
a temperature parameter (`src/utils/models.ts`), none is sent, and answers are sampled.

Two consequences.

**Routed versus control.** 23 discordant pairs of 199 is 11.6%, against a 7.8% floor from
identical inputs alone — about 15.5 pairs of 199 expected from sampling before selection does
anything. The -0.5 pp point estimate and its interval are unchanged and, if anything,
reinforced. What changes is the reading: most of those 23 questions are not places where the
budget mattered.

**Routed versus user-only decomposes, and the decomposition is the stronger result.**

| subset | n | routed wins | losses | ties | McNemar exact p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| identical prompt | 180 | 9 | 5 | 166 | 0.424 |
| different prompt (all single-session-assistant) | 19 | 16 | 0 | 3 | 0.0000305 |
| all | 199 | 25 | 5 | 169 | 0.000325 |

Every question on which the two arms rendered different turns is single-session-assistant — 19
of the 22 in the set, the ones matching the routing rule — and on those routed scores 18
against user-only's 2, sixteen wins and no losses. The remaining 14 discordant pairs come from
identical prompts and carry no information about the role rule. **16–0 on the questions the
rule actually touches is the number to quote, not 25–5 overall.**

## 6. Predictions against outcomes, and the stop rules

The predictions are the ones the design spec
(`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-03-role-aware-budget-and-episode-rerank-design.md`) stated
before the arms ran. Two are stated as approximations rather than bands; "about 690" is scored
held within 30 tokens and "about +9 pp" within 3 points, and both thresholds are this
analysis's rather than the spec's.

| prediction | predicted | outcome | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| routed overall accuracy | 86% (82–89) | 85.9% (171/199) | held |
| routed single-session-assistant | 15–17 of 22 | 20 of 22 | missed, above the band |
| routed multi-session | 44–46 of 53 | 44 of 53 | held |
| routed temporal-reasoning | 45–47 of 53 | 45 of 53 | held |
| routed recorded median context | about 690 | 672 | held |
| user-only overall accuracy | 77% (75–81) | 75.9% (151/199) | held |
| user-only single-session-assistant | 0–4 of 22 | 3 of 22 | held |
| user-only on the 177 non-assistant questions | 86% (84–89) | 83.6% (148/177) | missed, below the band |
| control on the 177 non-assistant questions | 84.7% | 84.7% (150/177) | held |
| routed minus user-only, overall | about +9 pp | +10.1 pp | held |

Eight of ten held. Both misses are informative rather than alarming: routing helped
assistant-directed questions more than expected, and user-only did slightly worse than expected
on the questions where it is supposed to be identical — the latter is within what the 7.8%
noise floor produces.

| stop rule | measured | fired |
|---|---|---|
| routed overall below 82% | 85.9% | no |
| routed single-session-assistant below 12 of 22 | 20 of 22 | no |
| routed recorded median context above 720 | 672 | no |
| user-only at or above 84%, which would falsify the role model | 75.9% | no |

None of the four fired.

Budget compliance is exact. All 199 routed questions and all 199 user-only questions came in at
or under 720 context tokens; both maxima are 716. The control, which ran with no budget, is
over on all 199 by design.

## 7. What the losses look like

Coverage below means whether the turns the dataset labels as containing the answer were in the
prompt the reader actually saw, matched by whitespace-normalised exact content. That
normalisation is load-bearing and was checked: over all 199 questions routed retrieves 359 of
360 gold turns under it but only 335 without, so 24 gold turns are whitespace variants a raw
compare would score as missing.

**Routed wrong where the control was right: 12 questions, two failure modes, six each.**

In six the budget cut gold turns that retrieval had already found. `88432d0a` rendered 1 of 4,
`ac031881` and `c4f10528` rendered 0 of 1, `gpt4_31ff4165` 3 of 6, `gpt4_a1b77f9c` 5 of 6,
`gpt4_f420262d` 1 of 3. The answers that came back either undercount — 3 bakes against 4, 3
health devices against 4 — or say the total cannot be determined. This is the real cost of a
720-token budget, and it is the mode that more budget would fix.

In the other six every gold turn was in the prompt and the answer was still wrong. Four of
those are single-session-preference, where the judge's complaint is not a missing fact but
advice that did not use the preference the rubric names. The remaining two are a wrong location
(`07741c45`, "under your bed" against a shoe rack in the closet) and a page-count sum the
reader declined to make (`37f165cf`). **These six are not budget failures**, which matters for
the single-session-preference cell in section 5: 4 of routed's 6 losses there had the evidence
in front of them.

**Routed right where the control was wrong: 11 questions, one dominant mode.**

Ten of the eleven are questions where the control's 15 retrieved turns did not hold every gold
turn — seven held some, three held none. Seven of those ten ask for a count, a sum or an age,
and the control either comes in short (2 tanks against 3, 1 museum against 2, 2 instruments
against 4) or reports that the total cannot be determined. This is a retrieval-depth failure,
not a budget one: retrieving 200 items and rendering 9 beats retrieving 15 and rendering all of
them, because the recall matters more than the render. The single exception, `d01c6aa8`, had
both gold turns rendered and answered "I don't know" while its own reasoning stated the values
the answer needed.

One gold turn is genuinely absent from routed's retrieval across all 199 questions
(`eac54add`); the closest retrieved turn scores 0.35 similarity, so that is a retrieval miss
rather than a text-matching artefact. Seven of the 199 questions carry no gold-turn label at
all, and none of them is discordant, so none reaches this section.

## 9. What this licenses, what it does not, and what to run next

**Licensed.** Ship routed selection at a 720-token budget as the default for this workload.
On 199 paired questions it holds the control's accuracy within a -0.5-point paired difference
whose interval spans zero, at 15.6% of the context. Keep the role-routing rule: on the 19
questions where it changes the prompt it wins 16 and loses none, and that comparison is clean
of the gateway confound because both arms ran minutes apart through the same gateway.

**Not licensed.** Three claims this run cannot support.

*Non-inferiority at a tight margin.* Certifying "routed is no more than 3 points worse than the
control" needs about **490 paired questions** before a dead-even result would merely clear the
margin — 493 by the arithmetic, using the discordance rate measured here (0.1156) and asking
for a 95% paired interval of half-width 3 points. That figure is a 50%-power threshold. To have
an 80% chance of clearing the margin when the two really are equal takes about **1,010** paired
questions, which is more than the benchmark's 500 questions can supply. What 199 pairs do buy
is protection against a large regression: at 80% power they detect a drop of about **8.2
points** (simulated, exact McNemar, two-sided α = 0.05, 40,000 trials, seed 20260904; the
normal approximation on the same model gives 7.9). So "routed did not fall off a cliff" is
established. "Routed is within 3 points" is not.

*A clean control comparison.* The control ran through a different gateway. Nothing in these
files measures a reader difference between gateways, so -0.5 points is a difference in
selection plus an unmeasured difference in gateway.

*Anything about questions outside these 199.* Each arm's own accuracy is pinned only to a
Wilson 95% interval of about ±5 points: control [81.0, 90.5], routed [80.4, 90.1], user-only
[69.5, 81.3].

**The next run, and what it will settle.** The routed arm over the remaining 301 questions is
already launched — run `memvara-routed720-500`, started 2026-09-04T07:51:48Z, resuming the same
199 evaluated results and adding 301, under identical settings
(`{"turnsOnly":true,"roleSelect":"route","headWhole":0,"tailChars":0,"tokenBudget":720,"searchK":200}`),
the same reader/judge id, and the same gateway. As of the last log line it was mid-search, at
roughly 46% of the 301.

It **will** settle whether routed's 85.9% and its context distribution hold on questions it has
never been scored on, and it will put the single-session-assistant advantage on a larger base
than 22 questions. It will also extend the budget-compliance check to 301 more prompts.

It **will not** settle non-inferiority, and this is the thing to be clear about: the control's
checkpoint holds exactly 199 target questions, so **there is no control on the other 301** and
no paired comparison can be formed there. The routed-versus-control question stays at n = 199
no matter how the 500-question run comes out.

**So the one run that matters next is the control over the same 301, through the arms'
gateway.** That single run does two things nothing else does: it takes the paired comparison to
500 questions, which is roughly the ~490 the margin arithmetic asks for, and by using the same
gateway as the arms it removes the confound that currently sits under every control-versus-arm
number in this report. Even then the result would be a dead-even-just-clears finding rather
than a powered one — 500 pairs is half of the ~1,010 that 80% power needs — and the honest
claim at the end of it would be "consistent with non-inferiority at 3 points", not "certified".

## 10. Anomalies, and what is unverified

| check | control | routed | user-only |
|---|---|---|---|
| questions with an empty context | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| questions over the 720-token budget | 199 (no budget set) | 0 | 0 |
| answer or evaluate phase failed | 0 | 0 | 0 |

No question in either arm rendered an empty context, so the role filter never discarded every
retrieved turn — the case the provider logs a warning for. All 2,985 phase records read
`completed`. All 597 judge records carry a non-empty explanation, a non-empty hypothesis, a
label of `correct` or `incorrect` (494 and 103), and no error wording. `promptTokens` equals
`basePromptTokens + contextTokens` on all 597 records, so the builder's clamp never clipped. The
checkpoint's copy of each question's search results matches the result file the answer phase
actually opens, on all 199 questions of all three arms.

Three things in this report are **not** verifiable from these inputs, and are reported on other
evidence:

1. **The gateway attribution.** It appears in no checkpoint field and in no arm log; the run
   script reads the base URL from `.env.local` and deliberately never prints it. The confound
   it creates is real, and its size is unmeasured.
2. **The $2.20 spend.** Read off the gateway's credit balance. The checkpoints hold token
   counts only.
3. **The progress of `memvara-routed720-500`.** Taken from its log and supervisor event file
   while it is still running, not from a finished checkpoint.
